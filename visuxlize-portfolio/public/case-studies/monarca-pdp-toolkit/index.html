Concept of design

Carry the brand to the buy button.

The design principle is simple. Trust and story are what sell wellness, so the product page should feel like the brand and remove every reason to hesitate. Practically, that means the buy box, price, and social proof sit above the fold, the subscription is framed as the normal choice, and the page stays fast and clean on mobile, where most of the buying happens.

The experiment

One clean test, run properly.

Testing is a discipline, not a guess. Here is the documented A/B test built into the demo, written the way a DTC team would actually run it.

HypothesisPre-selecting subscribe and save, instead of one-time, lifts the subscription rate without hurting overall conversion or refund rate. Most buyers accept a sensible default, so framing the subscription as the normal choice should start more of them.
VariantsControl A: one-time purchase pre-selected. Variant B: subscribe and save pre-selected. Split 50/50, with visitors held to one version across their visit.
Primary metricSubscription rate, meaning subscription orders divided by total orders.
GuardrailsOverall conversion rate, average order value, and 30-day refund or cancel rate. A win means the primary goes up while none of these move meaningfully against us.
Duration and stopping ruleDecide the minimum effect worth shipping before starting. Run until each arm hits its sample size, or a fixed two-week window, whichever is later. No stopping early on a lucky swing.
Reading the resultCompare the two arms with a two-proportion significance check. Ship the variant only if the lift is both statistically real and big enough to matter, with guardrails intact.

Honest note: a demo store has little traffic, so this shows correct methodology rather than proven numbers. The value is that the experiment is designed right and I can walk anyone through the reasoning. I would never dress up a thin sample as a real result.
